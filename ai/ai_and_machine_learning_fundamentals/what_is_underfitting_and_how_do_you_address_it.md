Underfitting occurs when a model is too simple to capture the underlying patterns in the data, resulting in poor performance on both training and test sets. The model has high bias and low variance—it makes strong assumptions about the data that don't hold. Signs include low training accuracy, low test accuracy, and the model performing similarly on both (no gap between training and test metrics).

To address underfitting, the first approach is to increase model complexity. For linear models, add polynomial features or interaction terms. For decision trees, increase max depth or reduce minimum samples per leaf. For neural networks, add more layers or neurons to increase capacity. The model needs sufficient representational power to learn the data's complexity.

Feature engineering is critical—underfitting often means the model lacks relevant information. Add new features, combine existing ones, or transform features to better represent the underlying relationships. Domain knowledge is invaluable here. For example, creating time-based features (day of week, hour) from timestamps can help a model learn temporal patterns.

Reduce regularization strength. Excessive regularization constrains the model too much, preventing it from fitting the training data. Lower the regularization parameter (lambda, alpha) or switch from L1 to L2 if sparsity is too aggressive. Train longer—insufficient training epochs can cause underfitting, especially in neural networks. Ensure the learning rate is appropriate; too high causes instability, too slow may prevent convergence. Finally, ensure the data quality—noisy or incorrect labels can make a problem appear harder than it is.
