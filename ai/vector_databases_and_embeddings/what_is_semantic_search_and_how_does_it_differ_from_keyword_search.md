Semantic search finds results based on the meaning (semantics) of the query rather than exact keyword matches. It uses embeddings to represent queries and documents as vectors, then finds the most similar vectors. A search for "affordable wireless headphones" would match documents containing "budget Bluetooth earbuds" because the embeddings capture the semantic similarity, even though no keywords overlap.

Keyword search (traditional search) uses exact word matching, typically with BM25 or TF-IDF algorithms. It counts how many times query terms appear in documents and ranks by relevance. Keyword search is fast, well-understood, and effective for exact matches—"iPhone 15 Pro" reliably finds documents containing those exact words. However, it fails when different words express the same concept, when synonyms are used, or when the query uses different terminology than the documents.

The key differences: semantic search understands synonyms, paraphrases, and conceptual relationships. "How to fix a broken screen" matches "repairing a cracked display." Keyword search requires the exact words to appear. Semantic search handles natural language queries better—"What should I do if my laptop won't turn on?" works naturally, while keyword search requires identifying the right keywords ("laptop won't turn on troubleshooting"). Keyword search is more predictable for exact term matching and handles proper nouns, technical terms, and specific product names better.

Hybrid search combines both approaches for best results. Use semantic search for understanding intent and finding conceptually relevant results, and keyword search for exact matches and specific terms. Implementations typically run both searches in parallel, then merge and re-rank results using a weighted combination of scores. This approach captures the strengths of both—semantic understanding for natural queries and keyword precision for specific searches. Most production RAG systems use hybrid search with re-ranking for optimal retrieval quality.
