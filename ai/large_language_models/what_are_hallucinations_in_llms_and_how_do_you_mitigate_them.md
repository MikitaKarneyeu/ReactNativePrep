Hallucinations are outputs generated by LLMs that are plausible-sounding but factually incorrect, fabricated, or nonsensical. The model generates text that doesn't correspond to reality or its training data, presenting false information with confidence. Types include factual errors (incorrect dates, names, statistics), fabricated citations (inventing non-existent references), logical inconsistencies, and confident confabulation (filling knowledge gaps with invented information).

Hallucinations occur because LLMs are probabilistic text generators—they predict likely next tokens based on patterns, not verified facts. They have no internal fact-checking mechanism. The model optimizes for fluency and coherence, not accuracy. During training, the model learns to generate plausible text, which means it can generate plausible-sounding but false text just as easily. Temperature and sampling parameters affect hallucination rates—higher temperature increases creativity but also hallucination risk.

Mitigation strategies include RAG (providing actual source documents reduces hallucination), grounding prompts with specific context ("Based on the following document..."), and chain-of-thought reasoning (forcing the model to show its work). Constrained decoding restricts outputs to factual templates. Multiple generation with consistency checking identifies hallucinated content by comparing outputs. Citation generation asks the model to cite sources, making unsupported claims easier to detect.

For production systems, implement verification layers: fact-checking against trusted databases, confidence scoring (asking the model to rate its confidence), human review for high-stakes outputs, and retrieval verification (checking if cited sources actually exist). Prompt engineering techniques like "If you don't know, say 'I don't know'" help but don't eliminate hallucinations. The most robust approach combines RAG, constrained output formats, automated fact-checking, and human oversight for critical applications. Hallucination remains an active research area with no complete solution.
