Fine-tuning adapts a pre-trained model to a specific task by continuing training on task-specific data with a lower learning rate. The pre-trained model already understands language—fine-tuning adjusts its behavior for your specific use case. This is dramatically more efficient than training from scratch: fine-tuning might require hours on a single GPU, while pre-training requires months on thousands of GPUs.

Training from scratch means initializing a model with random weights and training it on your data from the beginning. This requires massive amounts of data (billions of tokens), significant compute resources (hundreds to thousands of GPUs), and deep expertise in training dynamics. It's only practical for large organizations building foundation models. The resulting model learns general language understanding but isn't specialized for any particular task.

Fine-tuning approaches include full fine-tuning (updating all model parameters), which risks catastrophic forgetting—the model may lose general capabilities while learning the new task. Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods like LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) update only a small subset of parameters, typically adding trainable low-rank matrices to attention layers. LoRA can reduce trainable parameters by 10,000x while maintaining 90-95% of full fine-tuning performance. QLoRA combines LoRA with quantization for even lower memory requirements.

Instruction tuning fine-tunes on diverse tasks formatted as natural language instructions, improving the model's ability to follow instructions for unseen tasks. RLHF (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback) fine-tunes the model to align with human preferences, improving helpfulness and safety. In practice, most developers fine-tune pre-trained models rather than training from scratch. The typical workflow is: choose a base model (LLaMA, Mistral, GPT), prepare task-specific data, fine-tune with LoRA or full fine-tuning, evaluate, and deploy.
