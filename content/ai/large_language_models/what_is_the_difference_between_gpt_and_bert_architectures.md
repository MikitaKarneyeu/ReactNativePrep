GPT and BERT represent two fundamentally different approaches to pre-training language models. GPT uses a decoder-only architecture with causal (left-to-right) attention—each token can only attend to previous tokens. BERT uses an encoder-only architecture with bidirectional attention—each token attends to all other tokens in both directions. This architectural difference shapes their capabilities and optimal use cases.

Pre-training objectives differ significantly. GPT is trained with autoregressive next-token prediction: given "The cat sat on the", predict "mat". This naturally suits generation tasks. BERT uses masked language modeling (MLM): randomly masking 15% of tokens and predicting them from context. BERT also uses next sentence prediction (NSP). The bidirectional context gives BERT richer understanding of each token's meaning within its full context.

At small to medium scale, BERT excels at understanding tasks—sentiment analysis, named entity recognition, question answering—while GPT excels at generation. However, at very large scale (billions of parameters), the distinction blurs. GPT-3 and GPT-4 can perform understanding tasks through in-context learning and prompting, often matching or exceeding fine-tuned BERT models. This has led the field to favor decoder-only architectures for general-purpose LLMs.

In practice, encoder models (BERT family) remain valuable for embeddings, classification, and information retrieval where bidirectional context is important. Decoder models (GPT family) dominate for generation, conversation, and general-purpose AI assistants. Encoder-decoder models (T5, BART) are used for tasks requiring both understanding and generation (translation, summarization). Modern LLMs are predominantly decoder-only, with encoder models used primarily for specialized understanding tasks and as embedding models.
