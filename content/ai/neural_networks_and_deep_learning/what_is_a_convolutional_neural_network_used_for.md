Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) are primarily used for tasks involving spatial or visual data. Image classification is the foundational task—assigning a label to an entire image (cat, dog, car). CNNs learn hierarchical features: early layers detect edges and textures, middle layers combine these into parts and patterns, and deep layers recognize complete objects. Architectures like VGG, ResNet, Inception, and EfficientNet have achieved superhuman accuracy on benchmarks like ImageNet.

Object detection extends classification to localizing multiple objects within an image, outputting bounding boxes and class labels. Models like YOLO (You Only Look Once), SSD, and Faster R-CNN use CNNs as backbone feature extractors. Semantic segmentation classifies every pixel in an image—fully convolutional networks (FCN) and U-Net are common architectures. Instance segmentation (Mask R-CNN) combines detection and segmentation, identifying individual object instances. These are used in autonomous driving, medical imaging, and surveillance.

Beyond traditional vision, CNNs are applied to video analysis (action recognition, video classification), where 3D convolutions or CNN-RNN hybrids process temporal information. In medical imaging, CNNs detect tumors, segment organs, and analyze X-rays, MRIs, and CT scans. In NLP, 1D convolutions process text for sentiment analysis and text classification—though Transformers have largely replaced them. CNNs also process audio spectrograms for speech recognition and sound classification.

In mobile and web applications, CNNs power real-time features like camera filters (style transfer, background blur), AR effects (face detection, body pose estimation), document scanning (OCR with text detection), and visual search (finding similar products). Lightweight architectures like MobileNet and EfficientNet-Lite are optimized for on-device inference. Transfer learning with pre-trained CNNs allows developers to build powerful vision features with small custom datasets—fine-tuning a pre-trained ResNet on a few hundred domain-specific images can achieve excellent results.
