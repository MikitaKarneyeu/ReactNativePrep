Word embeddings are dense vector representations of words in a continuous vector space, where semantically similar words are positioned closer together. Unlike one-hot encoding (sparse vectors with one 1 and rest 0s), embeddings are typically 100-300 dimensional dense vectors that capture semantic relationships. The key insight is that words appearing in similar contexts tend to have similar meanings (distributional hypothesis).

Word2Vec, introduced by Mikolov et al. in 2013, learns embeddings by predicting context words. CBOW (Continuous Bag of Words) predicts a target word from its surrounding context words. Skip-gram predicts context words from a target word. Both approaches train a shallow neural network, and the learned weights become the word vectors. The training objective forces the model to place words with similar contexts nearby in vector space. Remarkably, these vectors capture analogies: vector("king") - vector("man") + vector("woman") ≈ vector("queen").

GloVe (Global Vectors) by Pennington et al. learns embeddings by factoring the word co-occurrence matrix. It combines the advantages of global matrix factorization (capturing corpus-wide statistics) and local context window methods (Word2Vec). GloVe explicitly models the ratio of co-occurrence probabilities, which captures meaningful relationships between words. FastText extends Word2Vec by using subword information—each word is represented as a bag of character n-grams, allowing it to generate embeddings for unseen words.

Pre-trained embeddings are typically used as initialization rather than training from scratch. However, static embeddings assign one vector per word regardless of context—"bank" has the same vector in "river bank" and "bank account." Contextual embeddings from models like BERT and GPT generate different representations based on surrounding context, solving this polysemy problem. In practice, static embeddings are used for simpler tasks with limited compute, while contextual embeddings from pre-trained Transformers are the standard for modern NLP applications.
