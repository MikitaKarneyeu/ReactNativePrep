Embeddings are dense, continuous vector representations of data (text, images, audio) in a high-dimensional vector space. Each piece of data is mapped to a point in this space such that semantically similar items are positioned close together. For text, a sentence like "The cat sat on the mat" might be represented as a 1536-dimensional vector of floating-point numbers. The specific values aren't human-interpretable, but their geometric relationships encode meaning.

The key property is that distance in embedding space corresponds to semantic similarity. Two sentences about the same topic will have vectors close together (high cosine similarity), while unrelated sentences will be far apart. This enables semantic search—"How do I reset my password?" will be close to "Steps to change your account password" even though they share few words. The embedding model learns these representations by training on massive text corpora, learning to place texts with similar meanings or contexts near each other.

Embeddings capture multiple dimensions of meaning simultaneously. In a well-trained embedding space, the vector for "king" minus "man" plus "woman" is close to "queen"—the model has learned gender as a meaningful axis. Similarly, "Paris" minus "France" plus "Italy" is close to "Rome"—the model has learned country-capital relationships. These analogies emerge from the statistical patterns in training data.

For images, embeddings capture visual similarity—images of cats are close together regardless of breed, color, or pose. For code, embeddings capture functional similarity—different implementations of the same algorithm are close together. Multimodal embeddings (like CLIP) map text and images into the same space, enabling cross-modal search (finding images that match a text description). The quality of embeddings depends on the training data, model architecture, and training objective—larger models trained on more diverse data generally produce better embeddings that capture more nuanced relationships.
