AI applications raise significant privacy concerns because they often process sensitive personal data—conversations, location history, behavior patterns, health information, and biometric data. The volume and intimacy of data processed by AI systems create risks that exceed those of traditional software.

Data collection concerns include the scope of data gathered. AI features often require more data than users realize—voice assistants process all conversations, recommendation systems track all interactions, and personalization features build detailed user profiles. Users may not understand what data is collected, how long it's retained, or who has access. Implement data minimization—collect only what's necessary for the feature, and be transparent about collection practices.

Model memorization is a unique AI privacy risk. Large language models can memorize and potentially reproduce training data, including personal information. If a model is trained on emails or documents containing personal data, it might generate that data in response to other users' queries. Techniques like differential privacy, data deduplication, and training data filtering mitigate this risk.

Inference privacy risks arise when AI models can infer sensitive information from seemingly innocuous data. A model might infer health conditions from search queries, political views from social media activity, or sexual orientation from app usage patterns. These inferences may be accurate and unwanted—they constitute a form of surveillance even if the user never explicitly shared this information.

Mitigation strategies include: on-device processing (data never leaves the user's device), federated learning (training models across devices without centralizing data), differential privacy (adding noise to protect individual data points), data anonymization (removing personally identifiable information), purpose limitation (using data only for stated purposes), and data retention policies (deleting data after it's no longer needed). Compliance with regulations like GDPR, CCPA, and HIPAA is mandatory—implement consent mechanisms, data access controls, and the right to deletion. Privacy-by-design should be a core principle, not an afterthought.
