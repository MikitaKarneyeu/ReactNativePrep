Fairness in AI means that model predictions do not systematically disadvantage specific groups based on protected attributes like race, gender, age, or disability. However, "fairness" is not a single definition—different contexts require different fairness criteria, and some fairness definitions are mathematically incompatible with each other.

Demographic parity (statistical parity) requires that the positive prediction rate is equal across groups. For a hiring model, this means the same percentage of men and women are recommended. This is simple to measure but can be problematic if the underlying qualification rates differ between groups.

Equalized odds requires that the true positive rate and false positive rate are equal across groups. For a medical diagnosis model, this means the model is equally accurate at detecting disease in all demographic groups and equally unlikely to produce false alarms. This is often more appropriate than demographic parity when accuracy matters.

Calibration requires that among instances where the model predicts 80% probability, approximately 80% of both groups actually have the positive outcome. This ensures the model's confidence is equally reliable across groups. Predictive parity requires that the precision (positive predictive value) is equal across groups.

Measuring fairness involves computing these metrics on disaggregated data—splitting your evaluation set by protected attributes and comparing metrics across groups. Tools like Fairlearn, AI Fairness 360, and What-If Tool automate fairness measurement. The choice of metric depends on context: in criminal justice, equalized odds (equal false positive rates) is critical to avoid disproportionately flagging certain groups. In lending, calibration ensures the model's risk scores are equally reliable for all applicants. In practice, you often cannot satisfy all fairness criteria simultaneously—choose the criteria that best aligns with your application's ethical requirements and potential harms, and be transparent about trade-offs.
