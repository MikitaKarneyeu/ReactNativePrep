Ethical considerations in AI development span fairness, transparency, privacy, safety, and accountability. The primary concern is bias—AI models can perpetuate and amplify existing societal biases present in training data. A hiring model trained on historical data may discriminate against women or minorities if past hiring was biased. A facial recognition system may perform worse on darker skin tones if trained predominantly on lighter-skinned faces. Developers must actively test for and mitigate bias.

Transparency means users should know when they're interacting with AI, understand how decisions are made, and be able to contest outcomes. Black-box models that make consequential decisions (loan approvals, medical diagnoses, content moderation) without explanation are ethically problematic. The EU AI Act and similar regulations increasingly require transparency and explainability for high-risk AI applications.

Privacy is a fundamental concern—AI systems often process personal data (conversations, location, behavior patterns). Developers must implement data minimization (collect only what's needed), purpose limitation (use data only for stated purposes), and security (protect data from breaches). On-device processing, differential privacy, and federated learning can enable AI features while preserving privacy. Users should have control over their data and the ability to opt out.

Safety and harm prevention require considering how AI features might be misused or cause unintended harm. Chatbots can be manipulated into generating harmful content. Recommendation systems can create filter bubbles or radicalize users. Automated decision systems can cause real-world harm when wrong. Implement safety guardrails, content filtering, human oversight for high-stakes decisions, and kill switches for when systems behave unexpectedly. Accountability means establishing clear responsibility for AI decisions—who is responsible when an AI system makes a harmful decision? Document model development, testing, and deployment decisions. Establish incident response procedures for when things go wrong.
