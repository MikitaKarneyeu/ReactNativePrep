The EU AI Act is a comprehensive regulation governing artificial intelligence systems in the European Union, adopted in 2024. It establishes a risk-based framework that categorizes AI systems into four risk levels with corresponding requirements: unacceptable risk (banned), high risk (strictly regulated), limited risk (transparency requirements), and minimal risk (largely unregulated).

Unacceptable risk AI systems are banned entirely. These include social scoring systems (rating citizens based on behavior), real-time biometric identification in public spaces (with limited exceptions for law enforcement), subliminal manipulation techniques, exploitation of vulnerabilities (targeting disabled or elderly people), and emotion recognition in workplaces or schools.

High-risk AI systems are permitted but subject to strict requirements. These include AI in critical infrastructure (energy, water), educational access (exam scoring, admission decisions), employment (hiring, performance evaluation), essential services (credit scoring, insurance), law enforcement (evidence evaluation, recidivism prediction), migration (asylum applications), and democratic processes. High-risk systems must implement risk management systems, data governance, technical documentation, transparency measures, human oversight, and accuracy/robustness requirements. They must undergo conformity assessments before deployment.

Limited risk AI systems have transparency obligations. Chatbots must disclose they are AI. AI-generated content (deepfakes, synthetic media) must be labeled. Emotion recognition systems must inform subjects. Users must be told when they're interacting with AI.

Minimal risk AI systems (spam filters, AI-enabled video games, inventory management) face no additional regulation. The Act applies to providers placing AI on the EU market, regardless of where they're based. Enforcement includes fines up to €35 million or 7% of global annual turnover. In practice, developers building AI features for EU users must assess their system's risk level, implement required safeguards, and maintain documentation of compliance. The Act is influencing global AI regulation, with similar frameworks emerging in other jurisdictions.
