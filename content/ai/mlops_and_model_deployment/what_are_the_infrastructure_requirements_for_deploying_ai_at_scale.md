Deploying AI at scale requires infrastructure that handles high throughput, low latency, high availability, and cost efficiency. The requirements vary significantly based on the type of model, traffic patterns, and latency requirements.

Compute infrastructure for inference includes GPUs for compute-intensive models (large neural networks, LLMs), CPUs for lighter models (gradient boosting, simple classifiers), and specialized hardware (TPUs, inference chips like AWS Inferentia). GPU selection matters—NVIDIA A100 and H100 are for large model training and inference, while T4 and L4 are cost-effective for serving. Implement auto-scaling to handle traffic fluctuations—scale up during peak hours and down during quiet periods.

Serving infrastructure includes load balancers that distribute requests across multiple model server instances. Container orchestration (Kubernetes) manages deployment, scaling, and health checking. Model serving frameworks (TensorFlow Serving, TorchServe, Triton) optimize inference with batching, model pipelining, and hardware acceleration. For LLMs, specialized servers like vLLM, TGI, and TensorRT-LLM provide continuous batching and KV-cache optimization.

Storage infrastructure includes model storage (S3, GCS for model artifacts), feature stores (Redis, Feast for real-time feature serving), and databases for prediction logging and monitoring. Vector databases (Pinecone, Weaviate) are needed for embedding-based applications. Caching layers (Redis, Memcached) reduce latency and cost for repeated queries.

Networking and reliability include CDNs for serving model artifacts globally, multi-region deployment for low latency and high availability, circuit breakers for graceful degradation when dependencies fail, and blue-green or canary deployment for zero-downtime model updates. Monitoring infrastructure tracks latency, throughput, error rates, and model-specific metrics. Logging captures all predictions for debugging and retraining. Cost management is critical—GPU inference is expensive. Optimize with model quantization, request batching, efficient serving frameworks, and right-sizing instances. In practice, most teams start with managed cloud services (SageMaker, Vertex AI) and build custom infrastructure only when the managed services' limitations become binding constraints.
