Asynchronous communication is the backbone of effective remote work. My approach treats async as the default and sync as the exception, which requires writing clearly, providing context, and being patient with response times.

I write with the assumption that the reader has no context beyond what I provide. In an office, you can say "hey, quick question about the auth service" and rely on shared context. Async, I write: "I'm working on the OAuth integration (ticket #456) and I'm seeing a 401 error when the token expires. I've checked that the refresh token is being sent correctly (see attached log). The issue seems to be that the token refresh endpoint returns a 403 instead of a new token. Can you take a look at the auth service logs?" This gives the recipient everything they need to help without a back-and-forth.

I structure my messages for scannability. I use short paragraphs, bullet points, and bold text for key information. I put the most important information first—the request or question—followed by context and details. Long, unstructured messages are hard to parse and get pushed to "I'll read this later" (and often forgotten).

I choose the right tool for the right message. Quick questions and casual updates go in Slack. Technical discussions with lasting value go in GitHub issues or pull requests. Design decisions and architecture discussions go in shared documents (Notion, Confluence). Announcements go in a dedicated channel. I avoid using DMs for work-related discussions because they're not visible to the rest of the team and create information silos.

I set clear expectations about response times. I use phrases like "No rush—whenever you get a chance" for non-urgent items and "This is blocking me—could you take a look today?" for urgent ones. I also practice good async hygiene: I check and respond to async messages at the start and end of my workday, and I avoid expecting immediate responses from others. The goal is communication that's thoughtful, complete, and doesn't require the recipient to be online at the same time as me.
