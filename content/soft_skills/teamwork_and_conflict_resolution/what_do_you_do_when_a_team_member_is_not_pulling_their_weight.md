When a team member isn't pulling their weight, my first step is to understand why before taking action. There's always a reason, and jumping to conclusions—or going straight to management—usually makes things worse.

I start by observing patterns. Is this a recent change or a long-standing issue? Are they missing deadlines, producing low-quality work, disengaging from discussions, or all of the above? I also consider context: are they dealing with personal issues, struggling with the technology, unclear on expectations, or overwhelmed by workload? I've seen cases where someone appeared to be slacking but was actually stuck on a problem they were too embarrassed to ask about.

If I have a good relationship with the person, I have a private, empathetic conversation. I don't accuse; I ask. "I noticed the last couple of tasks took longer than expected—is everything okay? Is there anything I can help with?" Often, this surfaces the real issue. Maybe they don't understand the codebase, maybe they're dealing with something personal, or maybe they feel the work isn't aligned with their skills. The conversation gives them a chance to be honest without feeling attacked.

If the conversation doesn't resolve the issue, or if it's beyond my ability to address (e.g., skill gaps that need training), I escalate appropriately. I talk to the tech lead or manager, framing it as a team productivity concern rather than a personal complaint. I provide specific examples: "In the last three sprints, Alex has completed 40% of the story points compared to the team average of 80%, and two of their PRs required significant rework." I avoid subjective labels like "lazy" or "unmotivated."

Throughout this process, I check my own biases. Am I holding this person to a different standard than others? Am I accounting for the difficulty of their tasks versus mine? Am I expecting them to work the same way I do? Fairness and empathy are essential—sometimes the issue isn't the person but the situation.
