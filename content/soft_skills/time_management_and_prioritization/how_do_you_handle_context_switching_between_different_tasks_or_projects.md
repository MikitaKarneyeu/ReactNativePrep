Context switching is one of the biggest productivity killers in software development. Research suggests it takes 15-25 minutes to regain deep focus after an interruption. My approach is to minimize unnecessary switches and make unavoidable switches as efficient as possible.

First, I batch similar work together. If I have code reviews, meetings, and deep coding work, I try to group them: reviews in the morning, meetings in a block after lunch, and coding in uninterrupted afternoon blocks. This reduces the number of mental mode shifts. I use calendar blocking to protect my most productive hours for deep work—typically 2-3 hour blocks in the morning where I don't check Slack or attend meetings.

When I do need to switch tasks, I make the switch clean. Before leaving a task, I spend 5 minutes writing a "context dump": where I stopped, what I was planning to do next, any open questions, and relevant file paths or branch names. This is my "save state" so that when I return—hours or days later—I can reload my mental context quickly instead of spending 30 minutes figuring out where I was. I use VS Code bookmarks, TODO comments, or notes in the task ticket for this purpose.

For projects that require completely different mental models (e.g., switching from a React frontend to a Go backend), I try to schedule these on different days rather than switching mid-day. If I must switch within a day, I use a transition ritual: a short walk, getting coffee, or doing a quick unrelated task (like answering a few emails) to give my brain a reset before diving into the different context.

I also advocate for reducing unnecessary context switches at the team level. This means limiting work-in-progress (WIP), not assigning a developer to three projects simultaneously, and being thoughtful about when to interrupt someone for a question versus waiting for a natural break point. If I notice I'm context-switching too frequently, I raise it in the retrospective—it's usually a symptom of overcommitment or unclear priorities that the team should address together.
