When learning a new technology, I follow a structured approach that moves from overview to hands-on practice to real-world application. The goal is to build practical competence quickly, not to read every documentation page.

I start by understanding the fundamentals and the "why." Before diving into syntax or tutorials, I ask: what problem does this technology solve? How does it compare to what I already know? What are its core concepts and mental models? For example, when I learned React, I first understood the component model, the virtual DOM concept, and the unidirectional data flow—these three ideas explained 80% of React's design decisions.

Next, I build something real. I've found that tutorial hell—watching videos or following step-by-step guides without building anything—is the least effective way to learn. I pick a small project that's meaningful to me (a personal tool, a clone of an app I use, or a component for a real project) and build it with the new technology. This forces me to make decisions, hit problems, and find solutions—all of which build real understanding. I deliberately avoid copy-pasting from tutorials and instead type everything myself, even if it's slower.

I use documentation as a reference, not a textbook. I read the "getting started" guide and the core concepts section, then refer to the API reference as needed. I also read the source code of well-written open-source projects using the same technology—this shows me how experienced developers structure real applications, not just toy examples.

Finally, I solidify my learning by teaching. I explain what I've learned to a colleague, write a short internal document, or present it at a team meeting. Teaching forces me to organize my understanding and reveals gaps in my knowledge. I also intentionally revisit the technology after a week—spaced repetition helps move knowledge from short-term to long-term memory. The entire cycle (overview, build, reference, teach) typically takes one to two weeks of focused effort, after which I'm productive enough to contribute to real projects while continuing to deepen my understanding.
