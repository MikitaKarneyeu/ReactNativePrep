Midway through building a customer portal, the company made a strategic decision to switch from a custom-built backend to Salesforce as the CRM platform. This was a fundamental change—our entire data model, API layer, and integration architecture had to be redesigned. We had six weeks until the original launch date, and the change added significant complexity.

My first action was to assess the impact systematically. I mapped every feature we'd built or planned against the new architecture: which features were unaffected, which needed modification, and which needed to be rebuilt from scratch. I discovered that about 40% of the frontend code was reusable (UI components, routing, state management), 30% needed modification (API integration layer, data transformation), and 30% needed to be rebuilt (features that relied on the custom backend's unique capabilities that Salesforce handled differently).

I communicated the impact to stakeholders immediately. I presented three options: delay the launch by 4 weeks to fully adapt, launch on time with a reduced feature set, or launch on time with a hybrid approach (some features hitting Salesforce, others using a temporary bridge to the old backend). The PM chose the hybrid approach, which let us meet the launch date while buying time for a complete migration.

I reorganized my work into three parallel tracks: adapting the reusable code, building the Salesforce integration layer, and creating the bridge for features that couldn't be migrated in time. I documented the technical decisions and trade-offs so the team understood why some features had temporary implementations.

We launched on time with the hybrid approach and completed the full migration two sprints later. The experience taught me that adaptability isn't just about writing new code quickly—it's about assessing impact, communicating clearly, and finding pragmatic solutions that buy time without sacrificing quality. The worst response to a major change is panic; the best is structured analysis followed by decisive action.
