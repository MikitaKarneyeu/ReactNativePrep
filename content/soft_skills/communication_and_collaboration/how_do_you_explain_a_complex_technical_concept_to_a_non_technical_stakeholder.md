When explaining complex technical concepts to non-technical stakeholders, I focus on translating technical details into business impact and relatable analogies. My approach has three key steps: understand the audience, simplify with analogies, and anchor on the "so what."

First, I identify what the stakeholder cares about—usually outcomes, timelines, or costs—not implementation details. For example, when explaining why we needed to refactor our authentication system, I didn't discuss OAuth token flows or session management. Instead, I said, "Right now, our login system is like a house with one lock on the front door. We need to add deadbolts and window locks to meet security standards our enterprise customers require."

Second, I use the "layered explanation" approach. I start with a high-level summary in plain language, then offer to go deeper if they want details. This respects their time and lets them control the depth. For instance, I might say, "We're upgrading our database to handle 10x more traffic," and only dive into read replicas and sharding if they ask.

Finally, I always connect technical work to business value. Instead of saying "we need to migrate from REST to GraphQL," I say "this change will reduce the number of API calls by 60%, which means faster page loads for users and lower server costs." I also use visual aids—diagrams, before/after comparisons, and simple charts—to make abstract concepts concrete. The goal is never to dumb things down, but to respect the stakeholder's expertise in their domain while translating technical complexity into terms they can act on.
