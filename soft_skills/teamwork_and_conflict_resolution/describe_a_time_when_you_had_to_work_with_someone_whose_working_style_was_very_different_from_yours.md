In a previous role, I was paired with a developer who had a very different working style from mine. I tend to plan extensively before coding—writing out the architecture, defining interfaces, and thinking through edge cases before writing a line of code. My colleague was a "code first, think later" developer who preferred to start building immediately and iterate quickly. This difference initially caused friction: I felt the code was disorganized, and they felt I was over-engineering and slowing things down.

The situation came to a head when we were building a data pipeline together. I wanted to design the data flow and error handling strategy upfront; they wanted to start with a working prototype and refine it. We were blocking each other—I was waiting for the design to be finalized before coding, and they were frustrated that I was "writing documents instead of code."

I suggested we have an honest conversation about our working styles. We both acknowledged that neither approach was wrong—they had different strengths. Planning upfront reduces rework; prototyping quickly surfaces unknown issues early. We agreed on a hybrid approach: spend one day on lightweight planning (a shared document outlining the main components and interfaces), then prototype for two days, and then regroup to solidify the design based on what we learned from the prototype.

This worked remarkably well. The prototype revealed a constraint we hadn't anticipated in the planning phase (the data format varied by source in ways the spec didn't document), and the upfront planning prevented the prototype from becoming production code without proper error handling. We shipped the feature on time and with a design we both felt good about.

The lesson was that different working styles aren't problems to solve—they're perspectives to leverage. The key was having an explicit conversation about the difference, finding a compromise that used the strengths of both approaches, and respecting each other's contributions rather than trying to convert them to your style.
