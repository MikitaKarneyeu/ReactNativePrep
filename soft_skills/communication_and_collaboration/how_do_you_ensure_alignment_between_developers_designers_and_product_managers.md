Alignment between developers, designers, and product managers requires structured communication touchpoints and shared artifacts. I rely on three practices: collaborative kickoff sessions, shared documentation, and regular sync points throughout development.

At the start of any feature, I advocate for a "three amigos" session where the PM presents the user story and business goal, the designer walks through mockups and user flows, and the developer raises technical constraints and feasibility questions. This catches conflicts early. For example, in a recent feature, the designer proposed a real-time collaborative editing experience, but during the kickoff, I raised that our current WebSocket infrastructure couldn't support it within the timeline. We collaboratively designed a phased approach: a simpler version for launch with real-time features planned for the next quarter.

I also push for a single source of truth—a shared document (like a Notion page or Confluence doc) that captures requirements, design decisions, technical constraints, and open questions. This prevents the "I thought we agreed on X" problem. I make it a habit to update this document after every discussion and share it with all three groups. When decisions change, the document is updated and everyone is notified.

Finally, I schedule brief check-ins at meaningful milestones rather than only at the end. A quick 10-minute demo after the first technical implementation, or a Slack message with a screenshot when the UI is taking shape, can catch misalignment before it compounds. I've found that the cost of realignment doubles with every week it goes undetected. By keeping designers and PMs in the loop through lightweight touchpoints—not just formal reviews—we maintain a shared mental map of what we're building and why.
