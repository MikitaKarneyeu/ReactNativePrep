I use a combination of synchronous and asynchronous practices to keep the team informed without creating communication overhead. The tools and cadences depend on the team's workflow, but here's my typical approach.

For day-to-day progress, I rely on our project management tool (Jira, Linear, or GitHub Issues) as the single source of truth. I update ticket statuses daily and add brief comments when I hit milestones or blockers. This means anyone—PM, designer, or fellow developer—can check the board and see where things stand without asking me directly. I also use Slack for quick updates: a brief message in the team channel when I complete a significant piece of work or encounter a blocker that might affect others.

For structured updates, I participate in daily standups and keep my update focused: what I completed, what I'm working on, and any blockers. I avoid turning standup into a status report by focusing on information that's useful to the team, not just my manager. For example, "I finished the API endpoint for user profiles. Today I'm working on the frontend integration. No blockers, but I want to flag that the API response shape is slightly different from what the design spec shows—I'll sync with Sarah about that."

For longer-term visibility, I use weekly written updates in a shared document or Slack thread. These cover progress against sprint goals, any scope or timeline changes, and upcoming work. I find written updates are especially valuable for remote teams because they're searchable, time-zone friendly, and don't require everyone to be in the same room. I also make a habit of proactively communicating risks early—if I see a deadline slipping, I flag it as soon as I know, not at the last minute. Early warnings give the team options; late surprises only create panic.
