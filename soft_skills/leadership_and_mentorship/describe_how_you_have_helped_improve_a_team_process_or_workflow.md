In a previous team, our deployment process was a source of constant stress and occasional outages. Deployments were manual, required coordination between two developers, and happened infrequently (once every two weeks) because the process was so painful. Failed deployments sometimes required rollbacks that took an hour to execute.

I proposed and led an initiative to automate our deployment pipeline. First, I documented the existing process step by step—I discovered that some steps were cargo-culted from years ago and no longer necessary. I identified the pain points: manual database migrations, environment-specific configuration, and lack of automated testing in the pipeline.

I designed a CI/CD pipeline using GitHub Actions that automated building, testing, database migration, and deployment to staging. For production, I added a manual approval step to maintain control while eliminating the coordination overhead. I also implemented blue-green deployments so that rollbacks could be done instantly by switching traffic back to the previous version.

I didn't implement this in isolation. I wrote an RFC (Request for Comments) and shared it with the team, incorporating their feedback. One developer raised a concern about database migration safety, so I added a migration verification step that ran smoke tests after migration before proceeding with deployment. Another developer suggested adding Slack notifications for deployment status, which I implemented.

The result was transformative. Deployment frequency went from biweekly to daily. Deployment time went from 45 minutes to 10 minutes. Rollbacks went from an hour to a single button click. But the biggest impact was cultural—the team went from dreading deployments to treating them as routine, which meant we could ship features and fixes faster. I documented the new process and trained the entire team, so I wasn't a bottleneck. The key to making it work was involving the team in the design, addressing their specific concerns, and showing measurable improvements that made their daily work better.
