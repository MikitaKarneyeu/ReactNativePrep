I led the migration of our authentication system from a monolithic session-based approach to a microservice-compatible JWT-based system. This was a cross-cutting initiative that affected every service and every team in the organization, and it needed to be done without downtime.

The initiative started because our company was moving to a microservices architecture, and the existing session-based auth couldn't scale across services. I was asked to lead the technical design and coordinate the migration across four teams. My first step was to thoroughly understand the current system—I read all the relevant code, documented the authentication flow end-to-end, and identified every service that depended on the session system.

I created a technical design document that outlined the target architecture, the migration strategy, and the risks. I chose a phased approach: first, implement the JWT service alongside the existing session system (dual-write phase); second, migrate services one by one to use JWT; third, deprecate the session system. This approach minimized risk because we could roll back any individual service migration without affecting others.

I coordinated with each team to understand their specific dependencies and concerns. One team had a mobile app that cached authentication tokens differently, which required a custom migration path. Another team had a tight deadline on a feature release and needed their migration delayed by a sprint. I adjusted the timeline and approach for each team while keeping the overall initiative on track.

The migration took three months and was completed with zero downtime and zero authentication-related incidents. I documented the entire process—including the gotchas and edge cases we encountered—so the knowledge wasn't locked in my head. I also created a reusable authentication library that standardized how all services validated tokens, reducing the effort for future services. The initiative taught me that leading a technical project is 30% technical design and 70% coordination, communication, and risk management.
