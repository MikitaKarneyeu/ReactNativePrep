Staying current in a fast-moving field requires deliberate effort without falling into the trap of chasing every new trend. My approach filters for relevance and emphasizes sharing with the team.

For staying current, I use a tiered approach. My primary sources are curated newsletters and blogs from trusted practitioners—sources like the React blog, MDN, and a few engineering blogs from companies whose technical decisions I respect (e.g., Netflix, Stripe, Vercel). I scan these weekly and deep-dive on topics relevant to my current work. For broader awareness, I attend one or two conferences or watch recorded talks per year, and I participate in a few online communities (Discord servers, Reddit) where practitioners discuss real-world challenges.

I evaluate new technologies through a practical lens. Before adopting anything new, I ask: does this solve a problem we actually have? Is it mature enough for production? What's the migration cost? What's the risk if it becomes unmaintained? I build small proof-of-concepts for technologies that seem promising rather than making decisions based on blog posts or hype. This keeps me informed without being distracted by every shiny new tool.

For sharing learnings with the team, I use several channels. I write short internal blog posts or Slack messages when I discover something useful—either from my own work or from my reading. I present at team tech talks when I have something substantive to share. I also make learning visible through my code and code reviews—when I use a new pattern or technique, I explain it in the PR description so reviewers can learn from it.

I also encourage a team culture of learning. I advocate for "innovation time"—a few hours per sprint where developers can explore new technologies or techniques relevant to our work. I create a shared bookmarks channel where team members can post interesting articles or resources. The goal isn't for everyone to know everything—it's for the team to collectively stay current and for knowledge to flow freely so that good ideas get adopted and bad ideas get filtered out through discussion.
