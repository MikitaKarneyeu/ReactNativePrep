The frontend ecosystem moves fast, and trying to follow everything leads to burnout. My strategy is to focus deeply on my core stack, stay broadly aware of trends, and learn new tools on-demand rather than speculatively.

For my core stack—React, TypeScript, and the surrounding tooling—I follow the official blogs and release notes closely. When a new React version is released, I read the changelog and try the new features. When TypeScript adds new utility types or syntax, I experiment with them. Deep knowledge of the tools I use daily has the highest ROI because it directly improves my productivity and code quality.

For broader awareness, I curate a few high-signal sources. I follow a handful of trusted voices on Twitter/X and subscribe to newsletters like "This Week in React" and "Frontend Focus." I scan these weekly—reading headlines and skimming articles that seem relevant. This keeps me aware of emerging trends (like the rise of server components, the shift toward edge computing, or new CSS features) without requiring deep dives until something becomes relevant to my work.

When I encounter a new tool or pattern that might be useful, I evaluate it through a practical lens. Does it solve a problem I actually have? Is it mature enough for production? What's the community size and maintenance activity? What's the migration cost from what I'm currently using? I build small proof-of-concepts for promising technologies rather than adopting them based on hype. Many tools that generate buzz never reach production maturity.

I also learn from my team and peers. Different developers follow different sources and have different experiences. When someone on the team suggests a tool or approach, I take it seriously because they've often done the evaluation work. Internal knowledge sharing—tech talks, pair programming, code reviews—is one of the most efficient ways to stay current because the information is already filtered for relevance to your specific context.
