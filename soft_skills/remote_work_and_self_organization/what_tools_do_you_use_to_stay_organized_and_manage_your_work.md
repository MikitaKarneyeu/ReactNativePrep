I use a layered tool system that captures work at different levels—daily tasks, sprint goals, and long-term projects—so nothing falls through the cracks.

For task management, I use our team's project management tool (Jira, Linear, or GitHub Issues) as the source of truth for all work items. Every task I'm working on has a ticket with a clear description, acceptance criteria, and status. I update tickets daily—moving them through statuses (to-do, in progress, in review, done) and adding comments about progress or blockers. This keeps the team informed and provides visibility to stakeholders without requiring me to give verbal updates.

For daily planning, I use a simple personal task list. At the start of each day, I review my tickets and identify the top 2-3 things I need to accomplish. I write them down in a personal note (I use Notion or a simple text file) and work through them in priority order. This keeps me focused even when Slack messages and meetings create distractions. At the end of the day, I note what I accomplished and what's next—this takes 5 minutes and makes the next morning much smoother.

For knowledge management, I use a personal wiki (Notion or Obsidian) where I keep notes on technical decisions, debugging techniques, architecture diagrams, and meeting notes. This is my "second brain"—when I need to remember why we chose a particular approach six months ago, or how to set up a particular service, I can find it quickly. I organize it by project and topic, and I review it periodically to remove outdated information.

For communication, I use Slack for real-time coordination and email for external or formal communication. I organize Slack channels by purpose—team channel for general discussion, project channels for specific features, and direct messages for quick 1:1 questions. I pin important messages and use threads to keep conversations organized. The key is not to let any single tool do everything—each tool has its strength, and using the right tool for the right purpose keeps things organized.
