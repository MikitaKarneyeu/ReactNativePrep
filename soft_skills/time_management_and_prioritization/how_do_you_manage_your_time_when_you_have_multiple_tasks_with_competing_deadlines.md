Managing competing deadlines requires a combination of prioritization frameworks, honest communication, and disciplined execution. My approach starts with clarity and ends with focus.

First, I get clear on the actual constraints. Not all deadlines are equally hard. A demo for a client next Tuesday is immovable. A sprint goal for "this week" might have some flexibility. I distinguish between hard deadlines (contractual, regulatory, or externally visible) and soft deadlines (internal targets that can be renegotiated). I also identify which tasks have dependencies—if Task B can't start until Task A is done, that affects my sequencing.

Next, I prioritize using a simple framework: impact multiplied by urgency. I rank tasks by their business impact and how soon that impact is needed. If two tasks have equal priority, I look for sequencing efficiencies—can I batch similar work together to reduce context-switching cost? I also consider effort: sometimes knocking out a quick task first clears mental space and reduces the number of things competing for attention.

I communicate proactively with stakeholders. If I have three tasks due Friday and realistically can only complete two, I flag this on Monday, not Thursday. I present the trade-off: "I can complete tasks A and B by Friday, or I can complete all three by next Tuesday. Which would you prefer?" This gives stakeholders agency and prevents last-minute surprises. Most managers appreciate early transparency far more than late apologies.

During execution, I use time-blocking to protect focus. I allocate specific blocks of time to specific tasks and minimize context-switching within those blocks. I turn off Slack notifications during deep work and batch my email/message checking. If I do get pulled into an emergency, I make a clean note of where I stopped on the current task so I can resume quickly. The goal is to be fully present on whatever I'm working on, rather than anxiously juggling everything at once.
