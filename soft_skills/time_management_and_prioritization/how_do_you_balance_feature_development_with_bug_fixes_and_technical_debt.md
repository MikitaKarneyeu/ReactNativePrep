Balancing features, bugs, and technical debt is one of the hardest ongoing challenges in engineering. My approach uses time allocation, prioritization frameworks, and transparency with stakeholders.

I advocate for a time allocation budget with the team and stakeholders. A common split is 70% features, 20% bugs and maintenance, and 10% technical debt and improvement. The exact percentages vary by team and product stage—an early-stage product might be 90% features, while a mature product with a large user base might need 40% maintenance. I discuss and agree on this split with the PM so it's an explicit commitment, not something that happens by accident.

For bugs, I use severity to drive prioritization. Critical bugs (data loss, security vulnerabilities, features completely broken for many users) get fixed immediately regardless of the feature plan. High-severity bugs are prioritized in the current sprint. Medium and low-severity bugs are batched and scheduled alongside features. I track bug metrics (open bugs by severity, time to resolution) to make sure we're not accumulating a backlog that will eventually become unmanageable.

For technical debt, I make it visible and tied to business outcomes. I maintain a prioritized technical debt backlog alongside the product backlog. Each item has a description, the impact of not fixing it, and the estimated effort. During sprint planning, we consider technical debt alongside features. I advocate for including at least one debt-reduction item per sprint. Small, consistent investment prevents the debt from growing to a point where it requires a dedicated "fix sprint" that derails feature development.

The key is making this balance explicit rather than implicit. If stakeholders only see feature velocity, they'll push for more features. If I make the cost of neglecting bugs and debt visible—through slower development velocity, increased incident frequency, or developer frustration—they're more likely to support a balanced approach. Regular communication about the state of the codebase, framed in business terms, keeps everyone aligned.
